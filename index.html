Attack Day

The Alliance Rule

Never farm a non-farming player or alliance. Everyone else is fair game. Expect to get farmed and expect to fight back.

  • Before you hit anyone, scout them first.
  • If they do not farm others, leave them alone.
  • If they farm, they are fair game. Expect them to come back at us.

Farming Rules

Farming means attacking another player’s in-game headquarters to take resources. A hive is a cluster of headquarters where members of an alliance stay together.

At most three VHDU members may farm the same hive at a time.

  • Do not zero a target’s headquarters.
  • Do not farm active players outside an alliance unless they farm.
  • Leave non-farming players alone.
  • If you see an alliance farming and it is not on the in-game alliance list, notify an R4 or the R5 for review.

PENDING The full target-eligibility rule still needs R4 or R5 confirmation. If you’re unsure a target is eligible, hold and ask. Do not post the target list here.

Your First Week in VHDU

A practice plan, not a required unlock schedule. Move at your pace and use what the game shows on your account.

  1. Day 1: Read the alliance rule and the current announcement. Find alliance chat and ask how the caller gives the go.
  2. Day 2: Pick one goal, such as your next HQ upgrade. Open its requirements and write down the next prerequisite.
  3. Day 3: Choose one formation to practice with. Compare the faction and skill descriptions shown in-game before spending materials.
  4. Day 4: Check hospital space, inventory, and any shield you plan to use. Read the shield details before you count on it.
  5. Day 5: Practice a scout report. Separate what the report shows from what you’re guessing. Wait if the target’s status is unclear.
  6. Day 6: Open the active event rules. If the caller has a role for you, join under their direction. If there’s no event, review an earlier battle report instead.
  7. Day 7: Write down one thing that helped and one question to test. Pick your next upgrade based on what held you back.

Handling "Tanks"

Tanks are enemy marches that come out of HQs to attack us. They are our biggest danger and our best chance.

  • Call it out fast. Post who is being hit, where, and how many troops are coming.
  • Reinforce. Send troops into the member being hit so the attackers face a bigger wall.
  • Counter-rally. An HQ that just sent its troops out is weaker. Rally it with the strongest players.
  • Protect the small. Newer and weaker members should shield up.
  • Keep hospitals empty and use shields when we are under heavy attack.
  • Counter the faction. Check the current matchup information and your battle reports.

Needs a test in game TEST Can we hit a march while it is on the map, and what does it cost? Share what you find in alliance chat.

Peace Day

Do not attack anyone. Only plunder your selected server.

  • Heal your troops and refill your hospital space.
  • Upgrade: HQ, heroes, tech.
  • Scout targets for the next attack day. Write down who farms and who does not.
  • Help newer members grow.
  • Pick the caller and the targets for the next fight.

SVS (Server vs Server) team roles

Grass Feeders

Grass Feeders: during SVS, keep a shield up and keep Auto Join on.

SVS means Server vs Server. During SVS, the team is split in two: the grass team and the MUD team. If you are on the grass, you are a Grass Feeder.

Two jobs. That is the least we can do for the alliance:

We are stronger when we work together, know the plan, and stick to the plan.

VERIFY Check the current shield and Auto Join rules on your in-game screen. The caller says who is on the grass and who is on the MUD.

MUD Team

MUD Team: keep the flag, hold the objectives, and fight in the mud.

The MUD is the muddy ground around the capital. The MUD team fights there for real.

  • Keep the flag.
  • Hold the objectives.
  • Fight in the mud around the capital.

The grass team keeps shields up and Auto Join on. The MUD team holds the line. Both teams follow the same plan.

VERIFY The caller says who is on the MUD, which objectives to hold, and who keeps the flag. Check the current SVS rules on your in-game screen.

What belongs in the Lessons Learned log?

A confirmed in-game lesson should name the game mode, what was tried, what the report showed, and what to repeat next time. Leave names, coordinates, member stats and screenshots with private details out of public notes.
